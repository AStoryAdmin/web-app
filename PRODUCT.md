# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Adult children, roughly 25 to 45, who want to keep what their parents and grandparents say before it is gone. They set the account up, invite the family, and are the ones who read. Their parents (65 to 90) are the storytellers: they answer questions by phone call or by writing, and some of them use this web app too, on a laptop at the kitchen table in the evening. Other relatives contribute memories and photographs through a code or a link without an account.

## Product Purpose

A Story asks a person about their life, one small question at a time, keeps their words and their voice, lets the family add what they remember, and turns it into a printed book. The web app is the signed-in home for that: today's question, the archive of memories, the family tree and invitations, and the book as it fills. Success is a first memory kept today and a first edition printed within a year.

## Positioning

The storyteller never has to type: A Story calls them on the phone and listens. The family does not have to organise anything: the questions come daily and the book makes itself from what was said, chapter by chapter. Nothing is ever behind a wall for reading, writing, inviting or contributing.

## Brand commitments

- Name and mark: "A Story", the A with the heart. The lockup colours are canonical (brand.json: terracotta and chocolate, gold heart).
- The printed book is real: teal bookcloth, ivory paper, Source Serif 4 for everything a person said. The serif is only ever for a person's words.
- Montserrat is the interface face (founder decision, Oct 2026).
- Memories keep their paper colours (chapter tint unless the family chose one).
- Copy: warm, plain, short. Never "legacy", "deadline", "before it's too late", "memoir", "immigrant". They/them for anyone unnamed.
- Same account and the same data as the phone app; the web can go beyond the app in design and navigation.

## Evidence

## Constraints

- Body text at least 16px, targets at least 44px: storytellers in their eighties use this.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion everywhere; nothing moves sideways.
- No sound.
- No trial language; no countdowns; no confetti.
