# Journaling and diary apps, 2026: what A Story should take

Research note for the founder, October 2026. Quoted phrases are the products' own words from their public pages and help centres; (reported) marks a fact taken from reviews rather than the product's own page.

A Story is not a diary. Its storyteller is 65 to 90, answers one small question a day by phone or in writing, the family adds what it remembers, and the book prints. So the question for each product was: what did they learn about writing, reading back, remembering, sharing and printing that survives the move from a private diary to a family book?

## 1. The field

**Day One** (web, Mac, Windows, mobile). Writing: "rich text formatting with markdown", "unlimited photos and videos", "voice transcription", a template Gallery. Version 2026.7 added Day One Gold ("Daily Chat", "go deeper" prompts, entry summaries, image generation) and a "Today" tab linking Entries, On This Day and "Moments" (calendar events, location history, photos as things to write about). Reading back: Timeline, Calendar grid, Media grid, Map, tags, search filters; "On This Day" lets you "take a trip back in time. No time machine needed", but is "not available on Web or Windows apps yet." Memory: streaks and reminders, no year in review. Sharing: Shared Journals for "up to 30 other people" by invite link, with comments and reactions, end-to-end encrypted, kept separate so "no entry in a private journal will ever be shareable"; pitched as a "digital family memory book". Print: Book Printing, 5.5 x 8.5 in, up to 384 pages, from $19.99, hardcover +$5, "A clean, content-focused design", choice of font, in-app preview; iOS only. Site: "Your journal for life." and "a layout that has everything you need and nothing you don't."

**Journey** (journey.cloud). Writing: paragraph styles, tables, checklists; photos, video, music; entries by email or WhatsApp; mood. Reading back: Calendar, "Atlas" (places), "Photo Search" into "meaningful moments and themes". Memory: "Throwback" from "a week, a month, or a year or two ago". Sharing: Shared Journal "with loved ones, family, or friends"; "Shared Web Links" opened "in a browser without the app"; end-to-end encryption. Print: "Cloud Print" to PDF ebooks; no physical book. Site: "Embark On Your Life Journey", emoji.

**Penzu.** Plain editor; covers and fonts per entry; "Saves as you go"; "Penzu Post" (write by email); Pro+ "Versions & entry recovery" and "fullscreen zen-mode". "Entry tagging" (Pro); "Links to your entries will be sent to you in the future." PDF export. "Your private 100% customizable online journal"; reviewers: "Dated interface".

**Reflection.app.** "100+ guided journeys", "one prompt at a time"; "Voice Journaling", a "Voice Call" that saves its transcript, "Scan Written Text" from handwriting; an AI Coach asking a follow-up "drawn from the entry you just wrote". "Ask Your Journal" answers "in plain language". "Weekly & Monthly Reviews" as "Auto-magical recaps"; the 2025 Annual Review produces "a first draft of your year", editable, shared by private link, billed as "Spotify Wrapped for Your Journal". "Private Share Links" per entry; "Full PDF, JSON & CSV exports". Site: "A journal that listens, and talks back", "Everything you need, none of the clutter."

**Goodnight Journal.** Public or private journals; members "Write public journals", "Comment on public journals", "Chat"; a "Monthly journal challenge"; "Turn your journals to a book" (grouping only); "Change journal date & time" and "Autosave journal while writing" are paid.

**Clearful.** "Discover Daily" hand-picked question; "Flow Text Editor" with "14+ Formatting Options Just 1 Tap Away". "Rediscover" shows entries from "1 Month Ago" to "3 Years Ago" and lets you remove one. Reviews with "streaks, stats, highlights, and quotes". Per-entry "Entry Lock". Web app. No sharing, export or print.

**Diarium** (Windows first). Entries carry "photos, videos, audio recordings, locations, tags, ratings and people"; weather and calendar integrations; "On this day" widget (reported). Sync through your own cloud drive. Export Word, HTML, JSON.

**Diaro.** Search "by keyword" filtered "by date, tags, folder, or location"; Dropbox sync to "Diaro Online". Thin otherwise.

**Paperlike Pro Journal.** Not an app: a linked PDF for GoodNotes with "Dedicated spaces to track your habits and moods" and "Linked pages for jumping between days, weeks, and months". Its lesson: a journal can be a designed object, not a feed.

**Notion templates.** 2,640 in the Journaling category, nearly all free: one database, a date property, a template button, calendar and gallery views; a few add "On This Day" views by filter. Reviewers: "easy to tinker with the system instead of writing."

**LiveJournal.** Privacy levels Public, Friends Only, Private, "custom friends groups", "comment screening". "Memories" is a keyword archive of favourite entries, including other people's, whose security is separate from the entry's. Calendar/Archive view; full export.

**Apple Journal** (iOS 26, iPadOS 26, macOS Tahoe). Two-pane Mac layout; multiple journals; sidebar "Insights" (calendar), "Places" (map), a "Bookmarked" filter; sort by "Entry Date or Moment Date"; print or Export to PDF. Photo suggestions stay iPhone-only; no "On this day" on Mac (reported). No web.

**Stoic.** "Start your day with a personalized check-in and wrap it up in the evening"; "Keep streaks, earn badges, and celebrate your milestones". Web via "Use in browser".

**Rosebud.** A conversation, not a page: "feels like texting a supportive mentor". Paid long-term memory learns "how different events and people connect"; "Rosebud remembers." It "isn't good with specific dates". Weekly reports.

**Mindsera.** "Minds leave margin comments on your writing like a collaborator"; generated artwork per entry; "Story View", the journal as "a scrollable narrative" with monthly and yearly summaries; "Ask Your Journal"; Voice and Call modes; web beta. Tone: "gym for the mind".

**Storyworth** (direct competitor). Weekly question by text or email from "500+ questions"; reply by email, "on any device, even a landline, no passwords necessary", or on the site. Paid voice: Story Calls transcribed word-for-word; "Magic Interviews" shaped into a "cleaned-up" narrative. "A built-in proofreader" working "without rephrasing or polishing their words". Relatives "read stories in real time", comment, and "those contributions can also appear in the final book"; each story is also an episode in a private podcast feed. Book: 6 x 9 in hardcover, up to 480 pages, layouts redesigned in 2025 by Carol Ly, photos "automatically resize and snap into place", one QR code to the year's recordings. Stories downloadable "for free at any time". Complaints: rigid photo placement, repetitive prompts, questions in spam, "one question per week" too slow for some and too fast for others. Site: all-caps headings, repeated BUY NOW, badges.

**Remento** (direct competitor). Weekly prompt by email and text, "an open-ended question or a photo". "No apps, downloads, or logins required": "Simply click the link and start talking", audio or video up to 30 minutes. "Speech-to-Story" yields "a word-for-word transcript or a flowing narrative"; "The original recording is always kept." Collaborators "follow along and react", suggest questions, add photos; "each family member can react or ask a follow-up". Book: 8 x 10 in colour hardcover, "about 50 to 200+ pages", "Every chapter has a QR code that plays back the original recording". $99 a year with one book, $69 a copy, archive kept for life. Site: "Stories on the page. Voice just a scan away.", Shark Tank badges.

**Two more.** Everlog (Apple): "beautifully minimal", you "start writing by tapping the quill"; users call it Day One "without the clutter". Life Note: "mentor replies" from historical thinkers, the far end of the AI-companion trend.

## 2. What A Story should take (ranked)

Each item: the feature, who does it best, why it fits, and how it looks in A Story's world (cloth and ivory sheet, Source Serif 4 only for what a person said, Montserrat for the interface, body at least 16px, no gamification, no confetti, no countdowns).

1. **The reply is the memory, from any channel.** Remento ("Simply click the link and start talking"), Storyworth (reply to the email, "even a landline"), Journey (entries by email and WhatsApp). The phone call is already A Story's answer; add the email reply and the relative's code link so nobody is asked to make an account to give a memory. In A Story: the question arrives as a letter in the inbox; whatever comes back lands on today's leaf, in serif, with a small note "kept from your reply".

2. **The family asks the next question.** Remento's collaborators "react or ask a follow-up"; Storyworth comments "can also appear in the final book"; Day One Shared Journals have comments. For A Story a follow-up is more than a comment: a daughter's "Which cousin was that?" becomes tomorrow's question, attributed to her. Under the family's side on the memory page, one quiet link: "Ask Dad about this." No reaction emoji; a name and a sentence.

3. **A photograph as the question.** Remento ("upload photos to learn the story behind each one"), Day One "Moments". Older people talk easily about a picture. A relative drops one in through their link; the storyteller's next call or sheet opens with it, large: "Who is in this photograph, and where was it taken?"

4. **The voice stays with the words.** Remento's per-chapter QR code "plays back the original recording"; Storyworth's single QR per book; "The original recording is always kept." A Story's calls already produce audio. Show a small ochre play mark beside the serif text on the memory page, and print one QR code in each chapter's running head. Caveat: one per chapter, never a sticker on every story.

5. **Word for word, or tidied, and the reader chooses.** Remento's "word-for-word transcript or a flowing narrative"; Storyworth's proofreader "without rephrasing or polishing their words". Default to their words with spelling fixed; offer "tidy this" as a second reading shown beside the first. Only the storyteller's own words are set in serif; a tidied version is labelled, never silently substituted.

6. **"When it was" beats "when it was written".** Apple Journal sorts by "Entry Date or Moment Date"; Goodnight Journal charges for "Change journal date & time". For a life story the moment date is the one that matters and is often vague ("the summer after the war"). Keep the "when it was" card, accept decades and seasons, and order each chapter by them.

7. **On this day, for the family, not the storyteller.** Day One ("take a trip back in time"), Clearful's "Rediscover", Journey's "Throwback". A diary resurfaces your own past; a family book should resurface what Grandma said a year ago to the people reading. On the family's Today sheet, one line in serif: "A year ago, Margaret told us: ...", with the chapter pill. No badge, no count.

8. **People and places as indexes.** Diarium's "people" field, Apple Journal's "Places", Day One's map and tags. A Story has a family tree; make every name in it a way to read ("Memories that mention Joe") and add a small map of the towns named. A cream card on the archive, pins as ink dots.

9. **The book you can already hold.** Day One's in-app book preview; Storyworth's "no design work"; Remento's "each story becomes a chapter". The Book page should show the real spread as it stands today, a page count in sentence case ("112 pages so far, four chapters"), and open any chapter as it would print.

10. **A chapter's introduction, drafted, then owned by the family.** Reflection's Annual Review is "a first draft of your year" that "you can edit any part of"; Mindsera's Story View writes yearly summaries. Draft a half-page chapter introduction from the year's memories, set in Montserrat (no person said it), signed "Written by the family", with an edit pencil. Caveat: no statistics, no "top memories", nothing resembling Wrapped.

11. **Saves as you go, and versions.** Penzu ("Saves as you go", "Versions & entry recovery"); Goodnight's paid autosave is a lesson in what not to charge for. An 82-year-old at a laptop must never lose a paragraph. Save silently, keep the last few drafts, and let the one "Kept" stamp stay the only ceremony.

12. **One memory, one private link.** Journey's "Shared Web Links" that open "without the app", Reflection's "Private Share Links". A Story has the wall at /p/:slug; add a per-memory link that renders the same cloth and sheet, read-only, with the family's side folded away unless the sender includes it.

13. **Favourites by any reader, kept apart from privacy.** LiveJournal's Memories, a keyword archive with its own security; Apple's "Bookmarked"; Everlog's notes on past entries. A reader marks "for the first page" or "read this at the party": a thin gold corner on the card, visible only to them.

14. **Download everything, always.** Storyworth: "download them for free at any time"; Remento keeps the archive "even if a subscription expires". Already A Story's rule ("Nothing is ever behind a wall"); say it on the Book page in one sentence and offer the PDF and recordings as one download.

15. **One way in, and "nothing you don't".** Everlog's "tap the quill"; Day One's "everything you need and nothing you don't"; Reflection's "none of the clutter". The writer is already two steps that never share a screen. Resist a toolbar: no headings, lists or colours on the storyteller's leaf. A paragraph break is enough.

## 3. Do not copy

- **Streaks, badges, challenges** (Stoic "earn badges", Day One "journal streaks", Goodnight's "Monthly journal challenge"). A missed day for an 85-year-old is a doctor's visit, not a failure. The book is the reward.
- **Mood, "state of mind", sentiment insights** (Journey, Stoic, Apple Journal, Reflection). A family book records what happened, not how the teller scored the day.
- **AI companions and coaching** (Rosebud "texting a supportive mentor", Mindsera "Minds", Day One "Daily Chat"). The listener in A Story is the family. AI may tidy a transcript or draft a chapter note; it never answers back in the storyteller's thread.
- **Generated artwork** (Mindsera). The photographs are the family's own; nothing synthetic goes in the book.
- **Public feeds, likes, anonymous community** (Goodnight Journal, LiveJournal). A Story's wall is one family's front door, not a feed.
- **Encryption as the headline, with user-managed keys** (Day One, Journey). Storytellers will lose keys. Be private by policy and plain language; keep "Private" to a lock on the card.
- **Formatting toolbars and markdown** (Clearful's "14+ Formatting Options", Day One). They suit writers, not tellers.
- **Per-entry covers, fonts and colours** (Penzu). The book has one typographic voice; chapter tints are the only colour choice.
- **Wrapped-style recaps and stats** (Reflection, Clearful's "streaks, stats, highlights"). Counts of words and days belong to habit apps.
- **Weather, fitness and calendar auto-fill** (Diarium, Apple Journal, Day One "Moments"). Today's weather is irrelevant to 1961.
- **A weekly cadence with "a year" as the clock** (Storyworth, Remento). Their users find it "too slow" or hard to keep up; a small daily question, with a book that prints whenever it is ready, is the better promise. Never show a countdown.
- **Sales chrome** (BUY NOW, Shark Tank badges, award ribbons). The old-money look wins by having none of it.
