# Atmosphere on the cream: what makes a page ask to be written in

Research note, 17 October 2026, after the founder said the signed-in sheet "looks a bit plain now" while scrolling, and that their own journal (journal.gbaonvo.com) is "even better". The question for each reference: which one or two devices make the page feel like a place while you scroll, and which one makes you want to start writing. Then what A Story takes, what it leaves, and what was built.

## 1. The founder's journal (journal.gbaonvo.com)

The site sits behind Cloudflare Access ("Project Reboot, private journal"); this agent did not sign in, so the notes below come from the founder's screenshots and description, not from a session.

What is on the page: a dark navy sidebar with a starry sky, origami cranes and a small mountain town; the founder's own illustrated character at a desk under a brass lamp; a red leather journal lying open on navy, ruled pages, gold corner ornaments, a ribbon bookmark hanging out below the book; "Dear me," at the top of the page and "Yours, me" at the foot, both in italic; "Questions for tonight" numbered i to iv; mood dots; a hand-drawn "How did today move you?" line chart in a pencil hand with notes like "Doubt" and "Restart"; a shiba and a samoyed walking along the bottom of the page; "Write it" as the first button; and the line "Progress isn't linear. Keep showing up."

Why it invites writing, precisely:

1. **The page is already a letter.** "Dear me," is written before you are. The blank is not a form field; it is the middle of a letter that has started. "Yours, me" closes it, so the space between is obviously yours to fill.
2. **The object is real and specific.** Leather, ruled paper, gold corners and a ribbon say "a journal", not "an app". The ribbon in particular says "you were here last time".
3. **The questions are few, numbered and for tonight.** Four, set like a list in a book (i to iv), with a time attached. That is an invitation with an end.
4. **A hand is present.** The pencil chart and its notes are someone's handwriting in the margin. A page that someone has already written on lowers the cost of writing on it.
5. **Something alive at the edge.** The two dogs walking along the foot of the page are the reward for scrolling to the end and the reason the page feels warm rather than designed.
6. **The verb is first and plain.** "Write it", not "Create entry".

What does not transfer: the navy night sky (A Story's world is the chocolate bookcloth and the ivory sheet; a starry night would fight it); the mood dots and the "how did today move you" chart (a grandmother's life is not scored); "Keep showing up" (a streak voice; a missed day for an 85-year-old is a doctor's visit).

## 2. The others

| Product | Atmosphere while scrolling | What makes you start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day One** (dayoneapp.com) | Short stacked sections, one idea each, image and text alternating like a slow tour; the closing section repeats the opening, so the page ends where it began. | "Tell your story, words optional." The promise that a photograph or a voice note counts. |
| **Stoic** (getstoic.com) | Soft illustration (an open journal, a bird beside a finished check-in); dated prompt cards in a repeating list give the page a daily rhythm. | A question as the last thing on the page ("how are you feeling?") beside the button "Start Journaling Now". |
| **Rosebud** (rosebud.app) | Full-bleed quiet photographs (a lake) between sections, used as rests rather than decoration. | "Speak freely" with a waveform: the barrier is lowered to talking. |
| **Reflectly** | Soft colour fields and one large friendly question per screen, conversational. | The question addresses you by name and is answered by tapping, not by facing a blank page. |
| **Apple Journal** | Almost none on the page itself; the atmosphere is the person's own photographs and places offered as suggestions. | A photograph from today offered as the prompt: "write about this". |
| **iA Writer** (ia.net/writer) | Focus mode: the sentence you are writing is dark and everything else fades; the screen is mostly typography. | "Make yourself at home": the blank page is presented as a room, not a test. |
| **Bear / Paper by WeTransfer** | The writing surface is the hero; texture, one accent colour, the tool as an object (Paper's pen and ink). | The pen lying ready on the page. |
| **Locket** | The friend's photograph arriving on the home screen: presence, not interface. | A picture from someone you love asks for a reply. |
| **Storyworth / Remento** (remento.co) | The book and its QR code, the storyteller's photograph, "How it works" steps. Sales rhythm: logo strips, repeated "Buy now". | "They just talk." "Try recording, it takes 60 seconds." The smallness of the first step. |
| **Apple product pages** | Sticky, scroll-scrubbed objects: the product stays while the words change around it. | Not a writing product; the lesson is the sticky object that changes with the section. |
| **The Pudding / NYT "Snow Fall"** | Scrollytelling: a sticky element (graphic, map) while steps of text pass over it; full-bleed images that fill as you arrive at them; a running sense of where you are in the story. | The reader is carried; the page knows where they are. |

## 3. What A Story takes

1. **The letter that has already begun** (the founder's journal). The leaf at the foot of Home and the Archive, and the writer's own leaf, open with "Dear family," and close with "Yours, Bao" (or "As told by Margaret"). The family book is a letter to the family.
2. **A ribbon** (the founder's journal, every printed book). A short silk ribbon in the cloth's colour hangs from the running head in the outer margin and lengthens a little as the page is read. On the writer's leaf its tail hangs out from between the pages.
3. **The running head that knows where you are** (printed books, The Pudding, Apple's sticky objects). The book's running head stays at the top of the sheet and names the section in view: "Recent memories", "1962", "What the family remembers", "A page for today".
4. **Ornaments drawn by hand as they arrive** (the pencil chart's spirit, in the book's foil instead). The ✦ rules between sections draw themselves from the centre as they come up the page.
5. **One question, already on the page, with the pen lying ready** (Stoic's closing question, Paper's pen, iA's "make yourself at home"). Today's question in the book's italic on a blank ruled leaf, a fountain-pen nib lying on the lines, one pencilled note in Caveat ("A few lines is plenty.") with a drawn arrow to "Write it".
6. **Something alive at the end** (the founder's two dogs). The family's watercolour puppy sleeps with its chin on the leaf's top edge and wakes, looking up, when you reach the end of the page.

## 4. What A Story does not take

- **No streaks or guilt.** No "Keep showing up", no days-in-a-row, no "you haven't written today". The note says the opposite: a few lines is plenty.
- **No mood scoring.** No mood dots, no "how did today move you" chart. A grandmother's life is not rated.
- **No starry night, no navy.** The world is the chocolate bookcloth, the ivory paper, foil, ink and the family's photographs.
- **No numbered list of questions to work through.** "Questions for tonight i to iv" becomes one question on the page; the hero keeps "Ask me another" for those who want more, and the Free plan's three a day are not undercut.
- **No progress bar dressed as a ribbon.** The ribbon is short (72 to 200px), lives outside the text column, and never measures the person.
- **No mascot behaviour.** The dog does one thing (sleeps, then looks up) in one place (the foot of the page). It never follows the pointer on the signed-in pages.
