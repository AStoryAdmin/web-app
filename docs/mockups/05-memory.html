The lake, the summer Dad learned to swim

Told by Rose on a call, Thursday 8 October · 6 minutes

Your father was forty years old and had never once put his head under water. He grew up on a farm, there was no lake, and by the time there was one he had decided it was too late. So that summer we rented the cabin on Green Lake, and Walt stood in the shallows every single morning with his arms out, like he was catching a child, and the whole beach watched.

The children

Dan was six and could already swim like a fish, and that was the trouble. He would not leave his father alone. "Just put your face in, Dad. Just your face." Walt would go red to the ears. I sat in the folding chair with my book and pretended not to look, which is what you do.

On the Thursday, with nobody near, he did it. Face in, then the whole head, then he came up spluttering and shouting and Dan was already running down the dock. I have never heard your father laugh like that before or since. The water was so cold it took your breath away, and neither of them cared.

He swam every summer after that. Not well. But every summer.
