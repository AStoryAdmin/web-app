# A very easy manual: A Story on the web

A Story is where your family keeps its stories. Someone talks, A Story listens, and what they say is kept in their own words and voice. The family reads it and adds what they remember.

Two parts: one for the person telling the stories, one for the person helping. Read the part that is you.

---

## Part 1 — If you are telling the stories

### Getting in

1. On your iPad or computer, go to the address your family gave you.
2. Press **Sign in**.
3. Type your email. We send you a six-digit code. Type the code. That's it. No password to remember.

You stay signed in. Next time, just open the address.

### Your home page

When you sign in, you see today's question. For example: *"What did summer smell like where you grew up?"*

Under it are two big buttons:

- **Talk** — you speak, A Story listens.
- **Write it** — you type.

Pick whichever feels easier today.

### Talking

1. Press **Talk**.
2. The first time, your computer asks if A Story can use the microphone. Press **Allow**.
3. A Story asks the question. Answer the way you would to a grandchild. Take your time. Silence is fine.
4. A Story asks one question at a time and follows what you say. If a question is not for today, say so. It moves on.
5. When you are done, press **That's enough for now**.
6. A Story shows you a short card of what you said. Press **Keep it**.

Your first ten minutes are free. After that, talking is part of a paid plan, and the page tells you the price. Writing is always free.

### Writing

1. Press **Write it**.
2. Type in the box. Spelling does not matter.
3. If you have a photograph, press **Add a photo**.
4. Press **Save**. It asks roughly when this was. A decade is enough. If you don't know, press **Save without a date**.

### Reading back

Press **Archive** to see everything you have told so far. Press any card to read it, see the photos, and hear your own voice if you talked.

### Things to know

- Skip any question. There is always another.
- Nothing you say leaves your family.
- You cannot break anything. If lost, press **Home**.

## Part 2 — If you are helping

This part is for the son, daughter or grandchild setting things up.

### Set up the story

1. Go to the address and press **Get started**.
2. Make your account: name, email, password. Or sign in with Google or Apple.
3. Type the six-digit code we email you.
4. It asks **Whose story is this?** Press **Someone in my family**, then their name and how they are related to you.
5. You are on Home. Write one thing you remember about them. That is the first memory. It takes a minute.

### Three different things, and which one you want

Read this before you press anything on **Family**. Getting it wrong makes two copies of the same person, and the phone app lets you.

1. **Your own story.** About you, in your words. You have one.
2. **A story you keep for someone who is not on A Story.** Your mother, your late grandfather. You do the typing; it is kept in their name, "as told to you". If they join A Story later, you can hand the story over to them.
3. **Joining the story of someone who is already on A Story.** Do **not** start a story about them. Ask them (or whoever keeps their story) for the six-letter code on their Family page, or the link. Then you read what they chose to share and add what *you* remember, under your own name. They approve it. You never write as them.

On **Whose story is this?** the third choice says exactly this: **Someone who is already on A Story**. Choose it, type the code, and you are in theirs. If their story is already kept in your family, it is listed there too with **Ask to join**; they say yes and you are in.

The storyteller button at the top of every page opens the same three, under three headings: **Your story**, **Stories you keep for someone**, **Stories you've joined**. Under them, **Keep someone else's story** (for someone who isn't on A Story yet) and **Join someone's story**.

### Get them in

Your storyteller needs their own way in. Two ways:

- **Email.** On **Family**, press **Add someone**. Type their name, how they are related, and their email. They get an email with a link. Sit with them the first time if you can.
- **A code.** On **Family** there is a six-letter code. Give it by text or on paper. When they sign up and type it, they are in.

### Invite the rest of the family

Same button. Anyone you add can read what your storyteller said and add their own version. Free for them, on every plan.

For a relative who does not want an account, press **Share a link**. The link opens a page where they can send a memory, a note or a photograph with no sign-in. You approve it before it appears.

### Adding people to the family, and when they see it

When you add a person on **Family**, only your family sees them, marked **not on A Story yet**. Send them an invite and it says **invited · waiting for them**. When they accept with the code or the link, it says **on A Story**, and from then on they see your family and you see theirs. Until they accept, nothing you typed appears on their side. If you add someone by email who is already there, A Story uses the person who is there instead of making a second one. If you add a name that is already listed, it asks **Is this the same person?** first. If someone is already listed twice, **Family** says so in one line, **Looks like X is listed twice**, and offers to merge them or remove the extra.

### Public, private, and the wall

Every memory is **Public** or **Private**. Public means the family on A Story can read it. Private means only you and whoever keeps the story. The switch is at the bottom of the memory page.

The **wall** is one page for one story that anyone with the link can read: the public memories and what the family added, nothing private, nothing still being written up. It is off until you turn it on in **Account → The wall**. Copy the link there and send it to a cousin or put it in the family group. Turn it off and the link shows nothing.

### Review what the family sends

When someone sends something, **Family** shows it under **Waiting for your review**. Read it, press **Add to the archive** or **Not this one**. Their name stays on what they sent. The same place shows when someone **asked to join** a story you keep (yes makes them a keeper, who reads everything and can edit; if you only want them to add what they remember, send the code instead) and when someone **suggests** a change to a person you look after. What you asked of others waits under **Waiting on others** until they answer.

### You can talk too

You can press **Talk** too. A Story knows you are the grandchild, and asks you about them, not as them.

### The book

Press **Book**. You see the whole book, page by page, as it stands today.

- **Print at home** is free, any time.
- Printed copies have their prices on the same page. If you have a plan, a Classic Hardcover is included each year; you pay shipping.

The book is never finished. Print one now, print another in five years.

### Plans

Everything above is free except A Story doing the talking. The first ten minutes are free. After that, a plan is $119 a year for one storyteller, or $229 for up to three. The price is on the page before you decide. Stop any time; the stories, recordings, family access and free book all stay.

### If the invitation email never arrives

The email is a convenience. The code and the link always work on their own, and they are made before any email is tried.

- **Where they are.** On **Family**, the sheet that said "X is in the family" shows the six-letter code, the link and a QR. Closed it already? Open the person on **Family**; their page has **Send the code** and shows the same code again. Every code you have made is also listed on **Family** under **Codes you've sent**, with the days it has left and a **Revoke** button.
- **What to send by text.** Press **Copy the whole message** and paste it into a text or a family group. It says who you are, gives the code on its own line, and the link. Or just send the six letters: they type them under **Someone in the family gave me a code** when they create their account.
- **How long it lasts.** A code works for 30 days and for one person. If it has run out, open the person on **Family** and send a new one; the page makes another.
- **If it says "The email didn't send: …".** The reason is in that line (no connection, the email service isn't set up, or an address that was refused). Nothing is lost: the code under it still works. Send it by text.
- **If it says "Sent" and they still have nothing.** Ask them to look in spam for a message from **invites@astoryapp.com**, and send them the code by text anyway.

The email's first button is only as good as the address A Story is hosted at. Until the web app is live at its own address, send the link you see on **Family** (it is this app's own address) rather than relying on the button in the email.

### If something goes wrong

- **No sound when talking:** check the microphone is allowed in the browser, and that the volume is up.
- **A relative can't see the story:** on **Family**, check they show as **on A Story**. If they show **invited · waiting for them**, send the email again or give them the code.

That's all there is. Ask one question today.
